# How do I report phishing emails pretending to come from EUSurvey?

## Intent / Description

Explains what to do if a user receives a suspicious or fraudulent email that appears to come from EUSurvey or the European Commission.

## Applies To

* Role(s): Any user (respondent, survey manager, external user)
* Feature: Security / Phishing awareness
* Context: Received suspicious email referencing EUSurvey

## Short Answer

EUSurvey never sends emails asking you to provide your password, banking details, or other sensitive personal information. If you receive a suspicious email that claims to come from EUSurvey or the European Commission and asks for such information, it is likely a phishing attempt. Do not click any links, do not reply, and do not provide any personal data.

Legitimate EUSurvey invitation emails always come from a `@ec.europa.eu` or `@eusurvey.eu` domain and link directly to `https://ec.europa.eu/eusurvey/` URLs.

## Steps / Procedure

1. **Do not click** any links in the suspicious email.
2. **Do not provide** any personal data, passwords, or financial information.
3. **Check the sender address** — legitimate EUSurvey emails come from `@ec.europa.eu` or `@eusurvey.eu` domains.
4. **Check the link** — hover over any link (without clicking). Legitimate survey links point to `https://ec.europa.eu/eusurvey/runner/...`.
5. If the email appears to be a phishing attempt, report it to your organisation's IT security team.
6. If you want to inform EUSurvey about the phishing attempt, visit the EUSurvey documentation page (https://ec.europa.eu/eusurvey/home/documentation) and open a support request describing the email.

## Important Conditions / Limitations

* EUSurvey does not have a dedicated phishing reporting form — reports should go to your IT security team first.
* EUSurvey cannot remove emails already sent to your inbox — that is handled by your email provider.
* If you are unsure whether a survey invitation is legitimate, you can verify by navigating directly to `https://ec.europa.eu/eusurvey` and checking if the survey exists.
* Some legitimate EUSurvey invitation emails may be sent by survey owners via their own email systems with links to EUSurvey — the key verification is the survey URL itself.

## Troubleshooting / Related Cases

* If you accidentally clicked a phishing link and entered your credentials, change your EU Login password immediately at https://webgate.ec.europa.eu/cas/.
* If you received a legitimate EUSurvey invitation but are unsure, contact the survey owner using the "Contact" link within the survey.

## Out of Scope / Separate Topics

* Reporting abuse within a survey (see: How do I report a spam or abusive survey)
* Technical problems accessing EUSurvey
* EU Login password reset

* Visit the EUSurvey documentation: https://ec.europa.eu/eusurvey/home/documentation
* If you cannot find your answer there, open a support ticket from that page.
