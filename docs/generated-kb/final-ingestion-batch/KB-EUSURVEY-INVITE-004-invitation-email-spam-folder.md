# Why EUSurvey invitation emails may go to spam or quarantine

## Intent / Description

Explains why legitimate EUSurvey invitation emails may be classified as spam by the recipient's email system, and what steps can be taken.

## Applies To

* Role(s): Respondent, Survey Manager
* Feature: Invitation emails
* Context: A participant finds their EUSurvey invitation in the spam or quarantine folder, or suspects it was filtered

## Short Answer

EUSurvey invitation emails may be classified as spam or quarantined by recipient email systems for several reasons:

- The recipient's email provider or organisation applies strict filtering rules.
- The email contains links, which some spam filters flag by default.
- The recipient has not previously received emails from the EUSurvey sending domain.
- Organisational quarantine policies automatically hold external emails for review.

This is not a fault in EUSurvey — it is a decision made by the recipient's mail system.

## Steps / Procedure

**For participants:**

1. Check your **spam**, **junk**, or **quarantine** folder.
2. Search for the survey owner's reply-to address or the word "EUSurvey" in the subject or body.
3. If found, mark the email as "Not Spam" or move it to your inbox.
4. If your organisation uses a quarantine system, check the quarantine digest or portal.
5. Consider adding the sender address to your contacts or safe senders list to prevent future filtering.

**For survey owners:**

1. Inform participants in advance (via a meeting, website notice, or other channel) that they will receive an invitation email, and suggest they check spam if it does not arrive.
2. Use a clear, recognisable subject line in the invitation template.
3. Avoid excessive formatting or multiple large images in the email body, as these may trigger spam filters.
4. Use the reply-to address field so participants can identify you as the sender.

## Important Conditions / Limitations

* **EUSurvey does not control recipient filtering**: The platform sends emails correctly, but cannot prevent recipient mail systems from classifying them as spam.
* **No sender domain customisation**: EUSurvey sends invitation emails from its platform sending infrastructure. Survey owners cannot change the sending domain.
* **Organisational mail policies**: Many government and corporate email systems apply strict filtering. This is outside EUSurvey's control.
* **No delivery confirmation**: EUSurvey cannot detect whether an email was quarantined or delivered to the inbox.

## Troubleshooting / Related Cases

* If multiple participants in the same organisation report missing emails: the organisation may have a blanket filter. Contact their IT department.
* If a participant consistently does not receive emails: consider providing the survey link through an alternative channel (e.g. directly in a message or document).
