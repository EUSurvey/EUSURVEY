# What should I do if my organisation receives suspicious EUSurvey-related emails?

## Intent / Description

Guidance for organisations that receive emails impersonating EUSurvey or the European Commission to collect credentials or sensitive data.

## Applies To

* Role(s): Any user, IT administrator
* Feature: Security awareness
* Context: Organisation received bulk suspicious emails mentioning EUSurvey

## Short Answer

If your organisation receives emails that appear to come from EUSurvey or the European Commission but seem suspicious (e.g., asking for credentials, linking to non-EU domains, or requesting financial information), treat them as potential phishing. EUSurvey will never ask for your password, banking details, or sensitive personal information via email.

## Steps / Procedure

1. **Do not click links** and do not forward the email to other staff.
2. **Verify the sender domain**: Legitimate EUSurvey emails come from `@ec.europa.eu` or `@eusurvey.eu`.
3. **Verify the survey link**: Legitimate links always point to `https://ec.europa.eu/eusurvey/...`.
4. **Report to your IT/security team**: Forward the email to your organisation's security team for analysis.
5. **Report to EUSurvey if appropriate**: Visit https://ec.europa.eu/eusurvey/home/documentation and open a support ticket to notify the EUSurvey team.
6. **Warn colleagues**: If the email was sent broadly, alert your team not to interact with it.
7. **If credentials were entered**: Immediately change the EU Login password and notify your IT security team.

## Important Conditions / Limitations

* EUSurvey **never** asks for passwords via email.
* EUSurvey **never** requests payment or banking information.
* Some legitimate EUSurvey invitations are sent by survey owners from their own systems and may not come from `@ec.europa.eu`. The key verification is the survey URL, which must be `https://ec.europa.eu/eusurvey/...`.
* EUSurvey has no control over emails sent by third parties impersonating the platform.
* Reporting to EUSurvey helps them track patterns but does not remove emails from your inbox.

## Troubleshooting / Related Cases

* If you are unsure whether an invitation is legitimate: navigate directly to `https://ec.europa.eu/eusurvey` and search for the survey.
* If the phishing email links to a convincing fake EUSurvey page: report to your IT team and CERT (Computer Emergency Response Team).
* If a staff member already entered credentials: change EU Login password immediately at https://webgate.ec.europa.eu/cas/ and enable 2FA.
